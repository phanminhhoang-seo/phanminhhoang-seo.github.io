Knowledge Graph facts go straight into AI Mode answers: triples, RDF and schema.org in 2026

Phan Minh Hoàng

SEO and GEO specialist, SEO Leader at Forza

Published 14/11/2026. 7 min read.

In late October 2026, two SEO researchers published evidence that Google’s AI Mode receives facts from the Knowledge Graph as a separate source, with no link attached. In other words, what Google “knows” about your business can go straight into an AI answer without anyone opening your website. This article explains the standards knowledge graphs are built on (triples, RDF, schema.org, Wikidata), what changed in those standards in 2026, and what role schema still plays.

1. What just happened with the Knowledge Graph and AI Mode?

On 28 October 2026, Dan Petrovic (DEJAN) published an analysis of Google Earth’s code (the build dated 15 October 2026), which bundles many of Google’s shared data structures. It builds on findings Natzir Turrado published the day before. Three points stand out:

  • AI Mode receives Knowledge Graph “biographies” as search results with no URL. Asked Petrovic’s age, AI Mode answered correctly and listed a “Person Biography” source with his birth year, birthplace, education and profession. For companies the equivalent is an “Organization Biography”: founding year, founders, headquarters, products.
  • Of the 9 source types recorded for AI Overviews and AI Mode, only 1 is a link to a web page, according to RESONEO’s summary. Another is content “generated from the Knowledge Graph”.
  • Each fact (a triple) carries a provenance record: whether it came from a third party, editorial curation, a partner feed or extraction from the web; whether it requires citation; and whether it is kept out of Gemini when a site blocks Google-Extended.

Read these findings with appropriate caution. This is reverse engineering, and Google hasn’t confirmed the details. The author himself notes that data structures present in the code don’t prove which facts carry which flags. What Google says publicly is that AI Mode uses “facts from our Knowledge Graph” alongside real-world information and shopping data.

2. What is a knowledge graph? How triples work

A knowledge graph stores information as entities and the relationships between them. The smallest unit is a triple with three parts: subject – predicate – object. A business is described by many triples:

SubjectPredicateObject
Company Ais aOrganization
Company Ais headquartered inHo Chi Minh City
Company Awas founded in2015
Company AoffersService X
Company Ais the same asCompany A’s Wikidata item

Two things make a triple different from an ordinary sentence:

  • Every entity has an identifier. “Company A” on the website, on Wikidata and in the Knowledge Graph must point to the same ID for a machine to know it’s one thing. Wikidata uses IDs like Q…, Google’s Knowledge Graph uses IDs like /m/… or /g/….
  • Predicates come from a shared vocabulary. “Headquartered in” must be a defined property, such as address in schema.org, not free text.

Entities in SEO are explained in more detail in Semantic search in SEO.

3. The standards behind knowledge graphs

StandardMaintained byRoleStatus in 2026
RDFW3CThe triple data model that the other standards build onRDF 1.1 is current; RDF 1.2 has been a Candidate Recommendation since 7 April 2026
JSON-LDW3CA way to write RDF data as JSON inside a page’s script tagJSON-LD 1.1 (2020), the format Google recommends
Schema.orgGoogle, Microsoft, Yahoo, Yandex and the communityThe vocabulary: entity types (Organization, Product…) and properties (address, founder…)Version 30.0 (19 March 2026), version 30.1 (16 September 2026)
WikidataWikimediaAn open knowledge graph anyone can read and editAbout 119 million items; Embedding Project and MCP access for AI since October 2025
Google Knowledge GraphGoogleGoogle’s own graph, a source for Knowledge Panels and AI ModeNot an open standard; websites can’t write to it directly

Put simply: RDF is the grammar, schema.org is the dictionary, JSON-LD is how you write it on a website, and Wikidata and Google’s Knowledge Graph are where the facts are stored.

The most notable new feature in RDF 1.2 is that a triple can be the object of another triple. That makes it possible to record “facts about facts”: who states this, since when, and from what source. Google hasn’t said it uses this standard, but it’s the same idea as what Petrovic’s analysis describes: every fact in the Knowledge Graph comes with its provenance and usage rights. A fact needs to be correct, and it needs to be traceable.

5. What does schema still do for AI?

Taken together, these events look contradictory: Ahrefs found adding schema didn’t increase citations, yet Knowledge Graph facts go straight into AI Mode. The way to reconcile them is to separate when schema is read, following Suganthan Mohanadasan’s framing:

StageWhat happensRole of schema
When Google indexes and builds the graphGoogle ties pages to entities and creates and updates factsClearest: precise data that’s easy for machines to read
When models are trainedWeb data is cleaned before it goes into a modelIndirect: JSON-LD is usually stripped, so the effect runs through entity stores
When AI answers a questionA bot fetches the page and reads it to answerSmall: a searchVIU test (October 2025) found 5 major AI systems ignored JSON-LD and read only visible content

Ryan Levering, a structured data engineer at Google, gave four reasons at Search Central Live Toronto (April 2026) why schema still matters: it’s more precise than model extraction for complex content such as pricing; it can express information not visible on the page; it’s cheaper than repeatedly inferring meaning from prose; and it focuses machine attention on what’s relevant.

The Ahrefs study has limits too: the pages in its sample were already heavily cited by AI before schema was added. It can’t tell us whether schema helps a new page get into an AI system’s set of sources.

6. What this means for businesses

  • Wrong facts can reach users with no link to check them against. If the Knowledge Graph has the wrong founding year or headquarters, AI Mode may repeat it and readers have no page to verify it. This is one of the causes covered in why AI gets your brand wrong.
  • Being mentioned doesn’t guarantee a link. Facts that live only in the graph can appear in answers without pointing to your website. That’s why measuring GEO has to count mentions, not just clicks.
  • No schema is better than wrong schema. Schema is raw material for triples. A wrong value, or a sameAs pointing to a namesake, puts a wrong fact into the graph. In Petrovic’s own test, the only citation AI Mode gave pointed to the Wikipedia page of a different person with the same name.
  • Blocking AI bots may affect facts too. The analysis shows facts can carry a Google-Extended opt-out flag, but the author says “may”, and Google hasn’t confirmed it. See types of AI bots and the AI training signal in robots.txt.

Which facts do Google and AI platforms use to describe your brand, and are any of them wrong? Checking entities and schema is part of how I measure a brand’s current GEO position.

FAQ

What is a knowledge graph?

A knowledge graph stores information as entities (people, organisations, products, places) and the relationships between them. Each fact is a triple of subject, predicate and object. Google’s Knowledge Graph and Wikidata are two well-known knowledge graphs.

What is a triple in a knowledge graph?

A triple is the smallest unit of fact, with three parts: subject – predicate – object, for example “Company A – is headquartered in – Ho Chi Minh City”. W3C’s RDF standard defines how triples are represented; JSON-LD and schema.org are how websites declare them.

Does adding schema help AI cite you?

There’s no direct evidence yet. Ahrefs’ May 2026 study of 1,885 pages found adding JSON-LD didn’t clearly increase citations in ChatGPT, AI Mode or AI Overviews. Schema matters more when Google ties pages to entities and builds its Knowledge Graph.

Is RDF 1.2 an official standard yet?

Not yet. On 7 April 2026 the two core RDF 1.2 documents became W3C Candidate Recommendations, meaning they’re technically complete and awaiting real-world implementation testing. RDF 1.1 is still the current standard.
