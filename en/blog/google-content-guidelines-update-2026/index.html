Google's October 2026 content guideline updates: main content, AI content and authorship

Phan Minh Hoàng

SEO and GEO specialist, SEO Leader at Forza

Published 13/11/2026. 2 min read.

In early October 2026, Google made a series of changes to its Search Central content documentation: defining a page's main content, requiring manual fact-checking of all AI-generated content, and warning against fabricated author profiles. The changes followed the September 2026 spam update. This article summarises each change and what it means for content teams.

2. Main content is judged on four attributes

Google defines main content as any part of a page that directly helps it achieve its purpose: text, media, interactive features, user contributions, titles and headings. Main content quality is one of the most important factors in assessing page quality.

The documentation describes four attributes Search Quality Raters look at:

  • Effort: how much human work went into the content or the systems producing it.
  • Originality: whether it offers unique information or perspectives.
  • Talent or skill: whether it shows the expertise needed.
  • Accuracy: whether it's factually correct, with higher standards for YMYL topics.

3. AI content: manual fact-checking required

The generative AI content guidance now stresses manually fact-checking and reviewing all AI-generated content for accuracy and trustworthiness before publishing. Google doesn't ban AI, but responsibility for accuracy sits with the publisher.

4. Authorship: no fabricated profiles

Google added a paragraph asking sites to avoid deceptive authorship information. Its examples: AI-generated headshots, made-up names or false credentials. According to Google, any form of deception makes a page untrustworthy to both users and automated systems.

  • Authors should be real, verifiable people.
  • Credentials shown on the page must be true.
  • Using AI to help write isn't deception; inventing an "expert" who doesn't exist is the problem.

5. What it means for SEO and GEO

The changes point the same way: Google favours content that real people stand behind, with original information that's accurate. Those same qualities help AI cite brand information correctly, as discussed in what is GEO and why AI gets your brand wrong.

Frequently asked questions

Does Google ban AI-written content?

No. Google asks publishers to manually fact-check and review all AI-generated content for accuracy and trustworthiness before publishing.

What counts as deceptive authorship information?

Google's examples include AI-generated headshots, made-up names or false credentials for authors. These make a page untrustworthy.

What is main content?

Any part of a page that directly helps it achieve its purpose: text, media, interactive features, user contributions, titles and headings.
