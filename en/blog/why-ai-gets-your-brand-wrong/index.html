Why does AI get your brand wrong?

Phan Minh Hoàng

SEO Leader at Forza, 4+ years in SEO and GEO

Published 13/11/2026. 1 min read.

Asking ChatGPT or Gemini about your own business and getting the wrong price, the wrong services or another company's details is not unusual. AI isn't lying on purpose: it answers from what it learned and what it can find on the web at that moment. This article lists six common causes and how to recognise each.

  2. Why this is worse than a wrong news article

1. Six common causes

CauseWhat happensHow to recognise it
Old training dataThe model learned from data up to a certain dateAI cites services, prices or addresses from years ago
Conflicting informationWebsite, social profiles and directories disagreeA different figure every time you ask
Too few independent sourcesFew external pages talk about the brandVague answers, or answers based on one forum post
Same or similar namesAI confuses you with a business or product of the same nameAnswers mixing details from two companies
Bots can't read the websiteContent needs JavaScript, pages fail or are blockedAI cites third-party sources instead of your site
The model "fills the gaps"With missing data, the model generates plausible detailsSpecific details that appear in no source

2. Why this is worse than a wrong news article

  • Nobody sees it to correct it: the wrong answer lives in each user's private conversation.
  • Users trust the answer: AI sounds confident, and few people double-check.
  • It repeats: one wrong source can feed many different questions.
  • It sends customers elsewhere: if AI says you don't offer what they need, they ask about someone else.

Frequently asked questions

Why does ChatGPT quote the wrong price for my service?

Usually because the model relies on old data or because prices conflict across different pages. When data is missing, a model can also generate a figure that merely sounds plausible.

Can AI confuse my business with another company?

Yes, especially when your brand name is the same as or close to another business or product and there is little information online to tell them apart.

Is it normal to get a different answer every time?

Yes. Language model output has a random element and depends on context. Conflicting information on the web makes the differences between answers larger.
