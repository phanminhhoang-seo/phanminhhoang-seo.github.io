Best agencies for improving entity relationships in organic search: 5 names and how to choose

Phan Minh Hoàng

SEO and GEO specialist, SEO Leader at Forza

Published 25/11/2026. 6 min read.

If you want help with how Google and AI systems connect your brand to its people, products, places and topics, five names keep coming up for good reason: Kalicube, WordLift, Schema App, iPullRank and Holistic SEO & Digital. They aren’t interchangeable. Each is strongest at a different layer of the problem, so the right pick depends on which layer is broken for you. This isn’t a ranking. At the end of section 2 I’ve added myself as an option for Vietnamese businesses, clearly marked as the author, so you can weigh that yourself.

1. What does improving entity relationships involve?

Search engines store facts as entities and the relationships between them: this company makes that product, this person founded that company, this article is about that topic. Improving those relationships means making every one of those facts clear, consistent and backed up. In practice the work falls into four layers:

  • Identity. One entity home, usually the About page, that says who you are, plus a stable ID in Google’s Knowledge Graph and ideally on Wikidata.
  • The graph on your own site. Schema.org markup where the organisation, founders, products and services are joined by @id, rather than a separate block on each page that nothing connects.
  • Content and internal links. Topics covered properly, with pages linked by the entity they’re about.
  • Corroboration from outside. Press, directories, partner sites and Wikidata stating the same facts you do.

Most agencies are very good at one or two of these layers. Triples, RDF and schema.org are covered in Knowledge Graph facts in AI Mode.

2.1 Kalicube

Jason Barnard calls himself “The Brand SERP Guy”, and Kalicube is built around one question: what do Google and AI say when someone searches your name? The company offers the Kalicube Pro platform, courses and bespoke services for knowledge panels. Its own stated KPIs are concrete, such as getting a Knowledge Graph ID within two to three months. This is the first place I’d look if the problem is identity: no knowledge panel, the wrong one, or AI mixing you up with someone else.

2.2 WordLift

WordLift was founded in Rome in 2017. It’s a platform first: it reads your content, identifies entities, links them to Wikidata and DBpedia, and publishes the structured data for you. It also builds product knowledge graphs from Google Merchant Center feeds, and has a WordPress plugin. For larger accounts the team handles setup. It suits sites where marking up thousands of pages by hand isn’t realistic.

2.3 Schema App

Schema App, co-founded by Martha van Berkel (CEO) and Mark van Berkel (CTO), treats schema markup as a data layer for the whole business, which it calls a content knowledge graph. The point is governance: on a large site, markup breaks every time a CMS, product line or team changes. If your problem is keeping layer 2 correct across a big organisation, this is the specialist.

2.4 iPullRank

iPullRank is a New York agency founded by Mike King, Search Engine Land’s Search Marketer of the Year for 2025. It describes its approach as “Relevance Engineering”: information retrieval, content strategy, UX and digital PR handled together, with its own tools such as Qforia for simulating query fan-out. It’s positioned for enterprise brands and has partnered with WordLift on knowledge graph work. Pick it when the problem is relevance across many queries and AI answers, not just markup.

2.5 Holistic SEO & Digital

Koray Tuğberk Gübür is known for topical authority, topical maps and semantic content networks, and he publishes case studies with real site names, including one on managing an entity’s identity. His methods have a large following and some loud critics, so judge the agency by case studies close to your niche. A disclosure: I use his semantic SEO framework in my own work, so read this entry with that in mind.

3. How to choose: questions to ask before you sign

  • Which layer is broken? Search your brand name and see whether a knowledge panel appears and whether its facts are right. Then ask ChatGPT and Gemini what your company does. The usual causes of wrong answers are in why AI gets your brand wrong.
  • What will they measure? Good answers are specific: a Knowledge Graph ID, a knowledge panel with correct facts, correct AI answers to a fixed list of questions. An “entity score” from the agency’s own tool tells you little on its own.
  • How does their own entity look? Search the agency and its founder. If Google and AI describe them clearly, that’s a small piece of evidence. If an entity agency’s own brand is muddled, ask why.
  • Do they promise a knowledge panel or a Wikipedia page? Nobody controls either. Wikipedia also requires paid editors to disclose that they’re paid.
  • What goes into your schema? Every sameAs must point to a profile that really is you, and every value must be true. No schema is better than wrong schema, because wrong schema feeds wrong facts into the graph.

4. Why most “best entity SEO agency” lists don’t help

When I searched this question in November 2026, several of the top lists were published by agencies that ranked themselves first. That pattern is now risky for the publisher too. In June 2026, Lily Ray analysed 100 B2B “best [category]” queries in Google’s AI Overviews. Across the 80 queries that showed an AI Overview, self-promotional lists were cited 323 times, and in 224 of those cases (about 69%) Google cited the brand’s own list but recommended a competitor from inside it. She also reported organic drops from around 20 January 2026 for sites that leaned heavily on these lists.

So this post doesn’t rank anyone, and where I’ve added myself, I’m last and marked as the author. Read every list like this, this one included, with one question in mind: what does the author gain from it?

5. If your business is in Vietnam

The methods carry over, but the sources that back up your facts are local: Vietnamese news sites, the national business registry, local directories, Google Business Profile, and Vietnamese-language pages about your products. Whoever you hire needs to work with those sources, in Vietnamese, or layer 4 stays empty no matter how good layers 1 to 3 are.

I do this work for Vietnamese brands: checking which facts Google and AI platforms hold about a brand, fixing the entity home and schema, and getting outside sources to say the same thing.
