A new AI training signal in robots.txt: Google will support it from early 2027

Phan Minh Hoàng

SEO and GEO specialist, SEO Leader at Forza

Published 13/11/2026. 2 min read.

On 30 October 2026, Google announced it will support a shared robots.txt signal that lets sites say whether their content may be used to train AI. The signal comes from the IETF's AIPREF working group and lets sites state one preference for many AI providers at once. Google plans to implement it in early 2027.

1. What is the AI training signal in robots.txt?

It's a "policy token" in robots.txt, listed in Google's crawler documentation as user-agent: __usage_ai-training. Instead of blocking each company's bots one by one, a site uses one signal to say whether its content may be used to train AI models.

  • Origin: a proposal from the IETF AIPREF working group; the IETF develops internet standards.
  • Status: still a work in progress, not a finished standard.
  • Google: adopting it voluntarily, planned for early 2027.

3. How it relates to Google-Extended

  • Company-specific controls keep working alongside it.
  • According to John Mueller (Google), if the shared signal opts out of training and the site doesn't specify Google-Extended, Google will treat it as if Google-Extended were disallowed.
  • Sites that allow Google-Extended can still appear in Gemini's grounded answers and receive clicks.

Existing bots and tokens are covered in types of AI bots.

4. How should businesses read this?

  • No change needed yet: Google won't apply it until 2027, and the proposal may still change.
  • It's a business decision, not just a technical one: opting out of training can protect content but may also mean models know less about your brand.
  • Training isn't search: this signal is about training, which differs from AI search bots reading pages to cite them, as explained in what is GEO.

Frequently asked questions

What is the AI training signal in robots.txt?

A policy token in robots.txt, from the IETF AIPREF working group's proposal, letting a site state once whether its content may be used for AI training, across many providers.

When will Google support it?

Google announced it on 30 October 2026 and plans to implement it in early 2027.

Does it replace Google-Extended?

No. Google-Extended still works. If the shared signal opts out and the site doesn't specify Google-Extended, Google treats it as if Google-Extended were disallowed.
